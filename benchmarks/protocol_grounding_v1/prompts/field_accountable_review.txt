Review the supplied protocol field values against their own visible source context.
Judge whether each complete value is supported, not established, contradicted, or
unassessed. Use the outcome definitions in the host instructions. Preserve the
supplied case IDs and value hashes and follow the output schema. Assess every case
independently; other candidate values are not evidence for this case. Do not assume
a distribution of expected outcomes.

Before assigning the outcome, check every factual clause in the bound field value,
including material identity, transformation, order, conditions, information effect,
scope and any qualification actually claimed there. A supported portion does not
license an attached unsupported portion. A citation must support the whole value
to justify supported. Identify the decisive unsupported or incompatible clause in
unsupported_clause when applicable; give a short rationale and inspected references.

Keep these distinctions explicit. An unreported detail is not established in the
provided context; that is not a finding that the experiment never did it. A
contradiction needs incompatible source evidence. A directly reported negative
observation can be supported, within its stated scope. An inference may be supported
when the provided premises and the reasoning warrant it: explain the connection
without importing experimental assumptions. Preserve possibility, comparison,
variant and observation qualifiers. A missing graph connection does not establish
the physical absence of a process. Do not strengthen uncertainty into a categorical
fact in your rationale. If you cannot decide, use unassessed. Do not add facts from
memory or other sources.
