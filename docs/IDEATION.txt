ATLAS-CONDITIONED ASSAY IDEATION
Implementation decision and reading guide — 5 November 2026

Purpose
-------
Given a research request, produce proposed genomics sequencing assay architectures
that can be inspected using the same State–Operation–Flow meanings as the atlas.
The output should make the proposed change, its precedents, its unsupported
assumptions and its decisive tests visible. It is not an automatically validated
invention, an executable laboratory recipe, or a claim of exhaustive prior-art search.

The atlas is a read-only source of reported workflows and, when loaded, reviewed
historical relationships. Proposed designs live in a separate overlay. A proposed
new connection must never silently become evidence about an existing technology.

What to read, in order
---------------------
1. src/genomics_discovery/ideation/run.py
   Request, ResearchPlan and pipeline define the stage order and saved artifacts.
   The runner owns the number of experts/revisions and checks outputs after every
   stage. It reuses execution/codex_worker.py for native model execution.
2. src/genomics_discovery/ideation/prompts.py
   build_prompt(role, context) supplies the exact plan/propose/literature/review/revise
   instructions. Context supplies file paths and tool commands, not the whole
   atlas or repeated paper text. Each actual stage_prompt.txt is archived.
3. src/genomics_discovery/ideation/models.py
   ProposedAssay contains research findings, the graph, design changes and assessment
   ledger. CandidateIdea is the preserved 1.0 format for reading earlier runs.
   IdeaReview records the closest prior work, issues and a bounded disposition.
4. src/genomics_discovery/ideation/context.py
   AtlasContext provides version-bound atlas lookup, bounded source reads and an
   audit log. This is the interface through which an agent conditions on the atlas.

One run
-------
Original request
  -> coordinator defines an agenda and two distinct expert mandates
  -> the two experts independently produce candidate assay graphs
  -> a separate literature investigator checks cited sources and searches closest priors
  -> a fresh reviewer sees both graphs and checks mechanisms, interfaces and prior art
  -> candidates marked revise receive the review and the other candidate
  -> each requested revision runs once, with a response to every review issue
  -> a fresh review checks revised graphs and tracks previous issues
  -> export the separate candidate graphs, reviews and evidence records

Two initial experts and one revision are prototype resource choices, not an
experimentally established optimum. The coordinator selects expertise appropriate
to the question; it does not assign the same fixed scientific specialties to every
request. Review has a distinct role and session. Calling two copies of a model
experts does not establish independent knowledge or substitute for expert validation.
The literature investigator has its own session and can research both drafts; the
design experts retain independent literature access. This role targets a concrete
failure mode: author-supplied citations may exist but fail to support the required
property, or may already describe the supposed advance.

The research bias is explicit before idea generation. The coordinator writes four
questions for EACH expert, tailored to the request and that expert's direction:
  capability: what useful measurement or improvement is missing in the closest
              existing assays, in which specimen and biological unit?
  mechanism: what experimentally demonstrated property could create the required
             distinction, including mechanisms from outside sequencing?
  interfaces: what material state must pass between operations, and what physical
              carrier preserves cell/molecule/location attribution?
  challenge: what competing method, confound or tradeoff could defeat the benefit,
             and what comparison would discriminate it?
These group the earlier project question bank's G01–G12 responsibilities; they are
our practical synthesis, not four laws established by the cited agent papers.
Experts choose queries, follow citations and can reframe a question with a reason.
They can explore biology or another engineering field when it changes a design
decision. Neither atlas membership nor combining named methods limits creativity.
Finding a paper and finding support for a precise property are separate tasks.

Interaction happens through concrete artifacts: the reviewer compares both designs,
and revision authors can inspect the other proposal and the critique. This is not
an unrestricted chat room or an assertion that multiple agents are always better.
Different useful architectures remain separate; there is no forced final merger.
If a stage fails, the run records that failure rather than pretending all experts
contributed. A failed or held proposal is not upgraded merely to fill the quota.

Request format
--------------
The request is a JSON object with prompt, constraints, preferences and origin.
The coordinator must preserve prompt and explicit constraints verbatim. Its own
interpretation and assumptions occupy separate fields. Preferences are not silently
converted into hard exclusions. A human or another LLM may supply the request.

Example request, not a demonstrated novel opportunity:
{
  "prompt": "Propose a sequencing assay that distinguishes newly synthesized from pre-existing RNA while retaining cell-level attribution. Identify a useful improvement over the closest existing assays.",
  "constraints": [],
  "preferences": ["Prefer equipment normally available in a genomics laboratory; disclose any new equipment requirement."],
  "origin": "human request"
}

This question is not hard-coded in the engine. An empty constraints list does not
mean there are no physical constraints. Physical requirements belong in the design
and its review; an unrequested equipment restriction should not be invented.

What a candidate contains
-------------------------
ProposedAssay (format_version proposed_assay/1.1):
  id, title, biological_question, measurement, expected_benefit
  research: capability, mechanism, interfaces, challenge
  workflow: states, operations, flows
  changes: earlier references, retained arrangement, changed arrangement,
           affected proposal records and expected gain
  assessments: evidence status and assumptions for every graph record/property

Each research finding has question, optional reframing_reason, conclusion, status,
affected targets, atlas/literature references and remaining_uncertainty. Status
evidence_found requires a located reference; it does not mean that the conclusion
is correct. Unresolved requires the missing evidence/question to be stated. The
software binds findings to the assigned questions and actual graph/change IDs.
Both literature and technical reviews must address these four findings. The graph
assessment ledger still addresses the scientific claims about individual records.
Concise findings should point to those records instead of repeating their text.

Version 1.0 candidates remain readable without inserting new fields or changing
their canonical hashes. New runs require 1.1 author outputs and ideation_run/2;
legacy plans execute only with their original frozen runtime. Archived contracts
can still be checked against their original schema. No old output is silently
upgraded or described as having followed the new research instructions.

State: the material preparation or dataset at a particular workflow boundary.
Operation: an action or inspection of particular input States with particular
           output States; it records input roles and effects.
Flow: an explicit material/data input, output or ordering dependency. It connects
      records by ID; it is not a historical-development relationship.

These reuse the atlas's meanings and property vocabulary. The proposal schema
deliberately does not reuse extraction's requirement that every proposed action
already be a reported action in a source. That would forbid the very innovation
we are asking for, or encourage fictitious evidence.

Instead, ClaimAssessment targets each State, State property, Operation and Flow:
  precedent: the required behavior is supported in the cited setting;
  transfer: that behavior is proposed in a different context, with an explicit
            compatibility assumption and a decisive test;
  hypothesis: the required behavior lacks support, with the unsupported property
              and a decisive test stated explicitly.

The status applies to the exact claim, not to the whole reagent or method name.
For instance, evidence that an enzyme acts on one substrate does not demonstrate
that it will act on a modified or immobilized substrate in the proposed workflow.
The existing enzymatic property and the proposed contextual transfer differ.
This example describes an evidence distinction, not a claim about a named enzyme.

Graph records have proposal-local IDs. References to the atlas use an existing
record_id, extraction_sha256 and node_id. A new architecture can have no whole-
method predecessor while still citing component precedents. Changes may affect
several States, Operations and Flows; no single-operation novelty rule is imposed.

The exported overlay (assay_ideation_overlay/2) represents each DesignChange as
one proposed_change node. earlier_basis edges connect exact atlas references to
that change; proposed_effect edges connect it to its affected proposal records.
This preserves a grouped before/after claim without inventing a separate mapping
between every earlier and later component. State-property targets retain their
property address. Flow targets retain their edge address through a flow_reference
node; that node is a pointer, not a new material State or assay Operation.
The original DesignChange and targets remain available in change_records.

How agents use the atlas and papers
----------------------------------
Each stage receives a tool_command with the pinned atlas, stage source registry
and audit log already specified. The agent appends a command:

  list                              browse the available methods
  search '<terms>'                  shortlist States/Operations by lexical terms
  workflow <record_id>              inspect one reported workflow
  node <node_id>                    inspect one State or Operation
  group <record_id> <operation_ids> inspect a connected group and its boundaries
  history <record_id>               inspect reviewed historical claims, if loaded
  claim <claim_id>                  retrieve one exact historical claim
  change-context <claim_id>         locate the exact before/after operation groups
  atlas-source <record_id> ...      inspect byte-pinned extraction sources/passages
  source-fetch <url> --title ...    fetch public source bytes and a reading rendition
  source-add <path> --url ... --title ... --content-level ...
                                    register actual retrieved text, not a summary
  source-read <source_id> ...        read bounded text windows or a literal match
  result-read <result_id> ...        continue an oversized tool response

Search is currently lexical candidate retrieval, including structured properties,
roles and effects. It is not semantic equivalence or a biochemical compatibility
algorithm. Agents inspect the selected records and can use external web search;
the atlas is not the boundary of permissible scientific knowledge.

change-context returns a navigation packet for a historical relationship, with
version-matched workflow bindings, affected Operations, boundary State IDs and
commands to read the full claim and groups. It does not summarize away scientific
qualifications. Similarity is not accepted as history; missing workflow versions
and external cited contributions remain explicit. This lets an expert investigate
what changed in an existing assay before proposing a different use of that change.

Large responses are stored with a continuation handle. Repeated evidence objects
are indexed once. This limits prompt growth while keeping exact records accessible;
it does not pretend a short summary contains every scientific detail. The source
registry preserves captured text and hashes; a literal quote match proves only
that the quote appears in the captured text. Agent-imported text still needs review
for source authenticity and whether it supports the scientific claim.
Repeated captures of identical URL/text bytes retain the original registration.
Different title or content-level declarations are recorded in a stage-local
capture_declarations.json sidecar; they do not create independent evidence or
silently promote an excerpt to full text. source-read and source-fetch expose
these declarations. No literal text match is a retrieval limitation, not proof
that the scientific evidence does not exist.
source-fetch preserves requested/final URLs, HTTP status, raw bytes and hash, text
rendition and hash. It accepts public HTTPS HTML, JATS article XML and plain text,
and PDFs when host pdftotext is available. PDF receipts retain the converter path
and version, original bytes and derived text. Empty/image-only PDFs and conversion
failures remain explicit acquisition limitations. Figures, chemistry diagrams,
tables and reading order still require inspection of the original when relevant.
It reuses jats_text
from the existing source pipeline. Its small HTTP downloader validates redirects
before following them; the older fetch helper did not expose that interception.
Address checks are not a substitute for managed network policy. HTML rendition can
omit figures/layout, and a successful fetch can return a login or challenge page.
The investigator must inspect it. Acquired text is conservatively marked excerpt;
a host receipt establishes origin, not complete full text or scientific validity.

A missing historical manifest is reported as not loaded. A missing edge, an empty
search, and an inaccessible paper are not proof that a capability is new or impossible.

Review and what the checks mean
------------------------------
Software checks exact IDs and graph connectivity, typed property/endpoint rules,
Operation input/output consistency, assessment coverage, referenced versions,
captured quotations, review binding and issue-response coverage. A fresh reviewer
must inspect the scientific meaning as well. Neither check proves a new assay works.

LiteratureReport separates citation identity from scientific entailment. It binds
findings to both exact candidate versions, checks each central measurement and design
change, and names other graph records inspected. Every distinct external source
used in the drafts receives a citation check. The investigator reopens the original
publication, compares bibliographic identity and actual wording/context, and stores
verification evidence; rereading an author's imported file does not suffice.
Metadata alone cannot verify a scientific passage. Claim verdicts distinguish
supported_in_cited_setting, transfer_not_established, contradicted and not_found.
The latter is a search result, not proof of impossibility. A verified_agent_review
citation status means an agent checked identity/capture, not that the assay works.
It additionally requires a matching source-fetch receipt with intact original and
extracted text bytes. The stage self-check enforces this origin requirement before
submission. Agent-imported text, including browser-only or image-only excerpts that
the host fetch tool cannot retrieve/render, remains unverifiable for citation origin; this is an
access/provenance limitation, not a finding that the paper is false. Reopening a
known URL and conducting a broader novelty search are checked separately.
Coverage limits and inaccessible sources remain visible. New claims/references
introduced in revision require fresh review; the old report cannot certify new bytes.

ReviewPanel records one IdeaReview per candidate, a comparison of the designs,
actual searches performed and access limitations. On a later review it records
previous_issue_resolutions. Unresolved issues must remain visible. The runner checks
that a non-incomplete novelty assessment has an observed external search execution.
Inherited searches only cover unchanged scientific candidate content; title/ID
cosmetics are excluded. Changed scientific content requires a fresh review search
or search_incomplete. This conservative comparison is not semantic equivalence.
An unresolved blocking issue cannot silently become minor; the reviewer must
explicitly resolve the original dependency before changing readiness. All previous
candidate, review, issue-response and stage-contract inputs are pinned. These
checks do not prove search relevance, coverage or correct scientific interpretation.

Novelty status is direct_overlap, possible_distinction or search_incomplete.
The review names the nearest prior work, the overlap and the residual difference.
It must not transform a search failure into a novelty claim. The disposition is
revise, retain_for_testing, hold or drop. retain_for_testing means retain for further
expert/experimental assessment, not an experimentally established technology or a
ready-to-execute protocol. Critical unsupported primitive properties and incompatible
interfaces must remain blocking issues until resolved or the claim is narrowed.
A specified, testable transfer of an experimentally established property can remain
conditional and be retained for testing. Lack of a completed experiment alone is
not a reason to reject every new architecture. A missing essential mechanism,
known incompatibility, unsupported required primitive behavior or uninterpretable
identity path instead requires repair or a hold. This distinction remains a
scientific review judgment, not a conclusion the graph validator can establish.

The distinction matters for our use case: the useful output is a proposed physical
measurement with inspectable interfaces, not only a biological explanation or a
more confident paragraph. The graph should let a scientist point to exactly where
a cell label is introduced, where a molecule changes form, and which proposed
transfer still needs evidence or a test.

Commands and artifacts
----------------------
Prepare/check can run locally. Native model execution belongs in a Qiu Sherlock
Slurm allocation under the existing project deployment instructions. Use the
selected frozen runtime and its environment; do not start local model jobs.

  PYTHONPATH=src python -m genomics_discovery.ideation.run prepare \
    --request REQUEST.json --atlas REVIEWED_ATLAS.json --output NEW_RUN_DIRECTORY

  PYTHONPATH=src python -m genomics_discovery.ideation.run run \
    --directory NEW_RUN_DIRECTORY

Optionally supply --history-manifest when preparing a run whose reviewed history
matches the selected workflow versions. Graph conditioning does not require every
catalogue entry to be present, but the run must report what context was available.

The run directory retains inputs/, config.json, state.json, plan.json, attempts/,
candidates/<expert_id>/initial.json and any revised.json/issue_responses.json,
literature_report.json, initial_review.json, final_review.json and sources.json. Each attempt keeps its
contract, exact prompt, schema, output, native trace and tool-call log. The input
atlas remains unchanged. For demonstration, show the request, selected precedents,
two candidate graphs, a concrete critique and the corresponding revision or hold.

Reference-implementation decision record
---------------------------------------
Contract fixed for this implementation:
- Observable inputs: original request, a pinned atlas, optional version-matched
  historical claims, actual retrieved literature, and artifacts from earlier stages.
- Output: proposed assay graphs plus traceable comparisons and uncertainties.
- Required invariants: proposal/evidence separation; preserved request; resolvable
  record IDs; explicit material/data routes; no unreviewed promotion into the atlas.
- Unavailable conclusions: experimental success, exhaustive novelty, optimal expert
  composition, or the superiority of graph conditioning without matched evaluation.
- Deployment: fixed bounded stage graph through the existing native worker; no new
  autonomous scheduler, unlimited debate loop or learned policy is introduced.

Material alternatives:
A. Put the whole atlas in every prompt versus retrieve relevant bounded records.
   Choose retrieval for inspectability and context cost. It can miss relevant work;
   external search and coverage reporting remain necessary.
B. Reuse the extraction schema unchanged versus separate proposed claim status.
   Choose shared scientific semantics with a proposal-specific assessment ledger.
C. Force a single combined idea versus retain distinct candidates through review.
   Choose independent candidates and optional cross-candidate refinement.
D. Score novelty from graph topology versus compare claims with retrieved prior art.
   Choose explicit prior-art comparisons; do not label absent edges as discoveries.
E. Install a separate hosted literature platform versus use current search tools
   behind an evidence-report contract. Use native web search, bounded source reads
   and captured provenance for the prototype; no PaperQA service is silently assumed.

What to test before claiming the atlas helps
------------------------------------------
Structural negative controls: an invented atlas ID; wrong extraction hash; a quote
absent from captured text; a material Flow connected to a data State; missing
assessment; a changed graph paired with an old review; an unanswered review issue;
and a novelty assertion with no observed search. These should fail predictably.
Also test wrong-publication citations, imported text that differs from the reopened
source, metadata-only access claimed as passage verification, and evidence reports
bound to an earlier candidate after revision. Origin and entailment must be evaluated
separately rather than combined into a single citation-validity score.

Scientific controls: a deliberately rediscovered existing assay, a familiar enzyme
placed at an incompatible interface, and two paraphrases of the same design. Check
whether the reviewer locates the actual issue and whether revision preserves it.
These require source/expert assessment; a software pass is insufficient.

First matched comparison: the same question, model, source access and approximate
budget with ordinary text evidence versus structured atlas evidence. Next compare
one proposal with independent proposals plus review. Keep the source snapshots and
evaluation criteria fixed. Record unsupported assertions, interface problems,
traceability, distinct physical design moves, latency and expert judgments.
Do not call structural validity, similarity to a historical assay or a model's
own novelty rating an innovation metric.

Remaining uncertainty
---------------------
This prototype's graph format makes proposals inspectable; whether it improves
scientific ideas remains an empirical question. Lexical retrieval may miss analogies.
Two experts may converge on the same idea, reviewers may share model blind spots,
and imported text can have provenance/interpretation errors. A single revision
cannot close every scientific gap. Keep those outcomes in the demonstration rather
than replacing them with an unsupported success claim. Native run status and test
receipts belong in the experiment directory and deployment status, not this design
record; this document does not assert a completed live ideation experiment.

Live-run corrections (5 November 2026; development version, not runtime_02)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
The shared prompt now describes the pipeline; DESIGN_OUTPUT applies only to
propose/revise. Coordinator, investigator and reviewer produce their own schemas.
Review may use observed searches from the dedicated literature stage for the exact
claims/versions checked; it must distinguish inherited work and research changes.

prepare and pipeline startup exercise the actual history reader, including linked
registries. The demo snapshot does the same. This prevents a valid-looking manifest
from reaching native agents when its sources cannot be resolved. Pair-level claim
and review evidence uses left:/right: namespaces; embedded extraction operation
structures keep their own source registries. The history reader checks the former
against the pair registry rather than recursively confusing those two scopes.
The exact live failure/replay and native observations are recorded in
provenance/experiments/ideation_demo_20261105/observation_02/REVIEW.txt and
history_replay/RESULT.json. Current live drafts did not obtain history context and
must not be presented as a successful test of historical-edge conditioning.
