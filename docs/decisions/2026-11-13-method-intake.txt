The intake unit is a distinct, experimentally demonstrated sequencing assay or
scoped assay implementation, not a paper. Cover post-2016 method introductions
and demonstrated assay workflow improvements, plus specifically verified missing
older predecessors needed to explain those methods. Do not promote every paper,
engineered reagent, software package, dataset, or search result to an assay entry.
Reagent/component papers can remain important sources for the mechanism, interface,
bias, performance, or development of a retained assay.

Screen abstracts before scheduling new full-text acquisition. Preserve the own
paper title, PMID/DOI, dates and date basis, exact discovery query memberships,
raw authoritative metadata receipt and hashes. Inspect the primary abstract for:
1. A sequence-based experimental measurement workflow and its measured subject.
2. An assay-level contribution: a new measurement/architecture or demonstrated
   change to the assay workflow, including a consequential library preparation
   improvement. Requiring a wholly novel architecture would be too restrictive.
3. Experimental assay evidence, distinguished from a proposal or analysis only.
Record yes/no/unclear, source passages, scientific rationale and reader/date for
each criterion. Terms such as method, reagent, atlas, multiplexed, or single cell
are discovery clues, never sufficient acceptance/rejection rules. An inconclusive
abstract becomes unresolved with a focused Methods/figure/supplement question.
It does not become a negative label. A focused document retrieval is justified
only by that recorded question, with an explicit bounded target list.

The scientific disposition is assay, component_only, application, review,
software_only, or unresolved. Record the publication contribution role separately:
primary_methods (including original software or reagent methods), later_protocol,
application, review, or unresolved. An atlas component/dependency role describes
how a paper is used for a particular method; record it on a separate method-source
association. It is not a bibliographic paper role. Legacy v1 fixtures retained the
older mixed field; v2 separates the concepts without changing the native judgments.
A supplement is a publication-bound document role, not a separately invented
assay or automatically interchangeable primary paper. A later protocol does not
replace a missing original article. A Methods citation can establish a procedural
dependency; its applicability and the implementation version still need review.

Resolve method identity before scheduling a new-method acquisition/extraction.
Use the existing canonical inventory/registry and original source bindings.
Check names/aliases AND own publication IDs; then decide same method/alias,
same implementation, supported distinct version, homonym conflict, or reviewed
absence from the pinned inventory. Name/identifier absence is a bounded catalogue
claim, not proof of semantic novelty or first invention. A distinct version needs
an explicit consequential assay difference with source evidence, not a changed
paper title or minor reagent substitution. One paper may propose several scoped
methods; one method may have several publications. Keep those many-to-many links.
For an older predecessor, also record which retained/newer method requires it and
the source-supported contribution. A date exception alone does not verify need
or absence. Keep unresolved older candidates outside new-assay acquisition.

Place staged candidates outside the live canonical library. Reuse the existing
publications and immutable source-vault bytes before new retrieval. Keep one
publication record per resolved paper identity, one document record per original
file/attachment, and a separately scoped method-paper association. Preserve exact
requested/final URLs, identity/association checks, original-byte hashes and text
conversion receipts. Keep raw metadata, abstract inspection, file availability,
source applicability, extraction, and independent review as separate statuses.

with assigned paper confirmation and explicit depiction/version differences.
