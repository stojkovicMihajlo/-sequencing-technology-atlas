What the graph must preserve about a sequencing-technology change
4 November 2026 — requirements audit, not new graph admission

Conclusion
The object to describe is a source-supported experimental change with an explicit
earlier arrangement, later arrangement, and consequence. State, Operation and
Flow are addresses in that description; choosing one node class is not a theory
of what an innovation is. The word 'concerns' is inadequate. A claim must say
which feature or process differs, from what to what, in which implementation.

Scope and completed work
Examined seven current extracted methods and targeted registered primary passages:
irCLIP, iCLIP, hiCLIP, icSHAPE, cP-RNA-seq, DR-seq and G&T-seq. G&T's own primary
registration is abstract-only; its detailed route remains supported by its
reviewed Illumina depiction, not a newly read primary full text. Also inspected
primary web descriptions for CEL-Seq2, SLAM-seq and Slide-seqV2. The last was
available as indexed abstract/passages; direct article opening hit a browser
challenge. No claim to have read all ten full papers or their supplements.
Nine comparison cases cover ten named methods, with baselines described where
supported. This is purposive requirements sampling, not an exhaustive taxonomy
or a scientific accuracy benchmark. cases.json records sources, limitations,
negative controls, extraction/source hashes and actual existing graph anchors.
All supplied local State/Operation anchors were checked against those bytes.
No primary-before-side historical link was admitted through this requirements audit.

Observable input and intended output
Inputs: registered Illumina depictions, scoped extracted workflows, actual primary
paper passages, citation contexts, source versions and acquisition limitations.
Output: a queryable account of what experimental arrangement was retained or
changed, what evidence establishes transfer, and what measurement consequences
were reported in the tested setting. Forbidden inference: an unreported mechanism,
a prior workflow invented from memory, missing meaning filled from a similar name,
or history inferred from temporal order or structural similarity.

What the cases teach us
1. CEL-Seq2: a library endpoint can stay similar while the route producing it
   changes. Required: distinguish a primer-borne sequence from a separately
   ligated adapter; preserve coordinated reagent and processing changes.
2. irCLIP: a reagent's different features can support different later operations.
   Required: connect a molecular feature to its specific use, separately from
   changes in digestion context, recovery and detection. One adopted component
   must not stand in for all the assay's innovations.
3. iCLIP: previously lost products can become the source of a measurement.
   Required: connect molecular origin, retention, library construction and the
   interpretation of the resulting read. A topology label alone cannot do this.
4. hiCLIP: a linker can change both a reaction route and interpretation of reads.
   Required: join identity, relevant end-state, processing order, and the meaning
   assigned to the joined sequences. 'Ligation' alone is not a comparison unit.
5. icSHAPE: an affinity tag and a sequenced reporter need not be on the same
   molecule. Required: preserve associations and release events; distinguish
   selected material from the molecule carrying the final sequence information.
6. cP-RNA-seq: intended target and physical selectivity differ. Required: state
   what property actually controls survival/capture and the known confounders.
   The current record accurately scopes three depicted end classes; that does
   not license a later agent to assert universal specificity over other ends.
7. DR/G&T: the same measurement goal can require different material routing.
   Required: preserve together/apart, separation versus aliquoting, order and
   branch identity. This comparison is not an asserted historical connection.
8. SLAM-seq: an initial molecular mark can become a different kind of observable.
   Required: distinguish chemistry, sequence signature and biological inference.
9. Slide-seqV2: reusable data can be necessary for the assay's physical output to
   mean anything. Required: retain array indexing and coordinate-map dependencies;
   do not model tissue processing as if it creates all necessary prior data.

Required scientific content in each method graph
These are questions with defined answers, not universal protocol stages:
A. Measurement: biological quantity, target population, unit, specimen and scope.
   RNA abundance, RNA synthesis during a pulse, and an RNA-protein contact are
   different quantities even when all produce RNA-derived sequence libraries.
B. Material identity at boundaries: actual preparations and consequential
   molecular features, attachment/association and compartment. A State is one
   routed preparation or dataset, not an arbitrary fact or name for an omics goal.
C. Experimental action: what acts on what, by what evidenced mechanism, producing
   what change or selection. Include reagent/condition/equipment distinctions
   when they establish that change, interface, applicability or reported result.
D. Arrangement: which preparations/data are inputs and outputs, where branches
   split or combine, and required precedence. Material routes and data dependencies
   remain distinct; a shared caption does not establish a physical join.
E. Information path: which feature records the biological distinction, how its
   association survives processing, and what instrument/data product exposes it.
   Sequence barcodes are one solution, not mandatory for every assay.
F. Interpretation and limits: how the observable supports the biological claim,
   which controls/maps/calibration are needed, and known non-unique explanations.
G. Evidence and performance: source support and unknowns; any performance statement
   names its metric, comparator and tested context. Measured outcome, author
   rationale and our mechanistic inference remain separate.

A-G map largely to existing fields. They do not justify rebuilding all States or
inventing a new seven-stage workflow. Capability/performance currently live mostly
in free-text scope and relationship fields, so they are less consistently queried
than molecular properties. Conditions and equipment can still be buried in prose.
Those are concrete consistency gaps, not evidence that all prior extraction failed.

Required content in an evolutionary relationship
Answer two separate questions:
  Origin: what source establishes that this later implementation adopted or
          developed a specified earlier contribution?
  Difference: what arrangement differs between the implementations being compared?
An imported component may be unchanged; an important new design move may lack a
named historical parent. Neither omission licenses inventing the other answer.

For each distinct documented change, record:
- the feature/process/arrangement being compared, stated specifically;
- earlier and later descriptions supported on their respective sides;
- what is retained, rather than implying the whole method is replaced;
- where the descriptions occur in available workflows;
- reported reason, measured consequence and limits, or not established;
- attribution and evidence that justify the relation type and direction.
One relation may carry several coordinated changes. Several contributors can
have separate relations to one later method. The same pair can have several
claims with different scopes. One component's adoption does not inherit its
entire enclosing operation, all downstream effects, or whole assay.

Keep relation types separate from descriptions of technical change
method_development/component_adoption describe historical assertions.
comparison/mechanistic_precedent/shared_mechanism/shared_purpose describe other
relationships. Replacing an enzyme, changing a handle, moving a reaction or adding
a data map describe differences; they do not themselves establish descent.
Do not add a historical 'improves' edge: outperforming an assay is not inheritance.
Derive cross-method change categories later from accepted descriptions; do not
force every observation into a new fixed taxonomy at extraction time.

Minimal representation revision supported by this audit
Keep the source-scoped State-Operation-Flow graphs and their existing properties.
Keep the current evidence/review gates and relation meanings. Replace operation-
only relation addressing with typed references to existing States, State property
assertions, Operations and Flows, as relevant. Keep these anchors distinct from
the actual scientific change description. Allow several anchors per claim.

A State anchor must specify the relevant property (e.g. a terminal group or
adapter feature); an Operation anchor must specify the action/mechanism/resource
or condition difference; a Flow anchor must specify the order/routing difference.
A whole-method capability claim refers to its implementation and evidence without
inventing a microscopic anchor. Unsupported or unextracted earlier detail stays
explicitly unavailable; do not fabricate node IDs. Retain source grouping unless
a distinction essential to this claim cannot be addressed without refinement.

This revision is NOT wired into the already frozen runtime05. Its irCLIP trial
still uses operation-only anchors and can return State-level facts in prose. It
can test citation tracing, not full structured State/property-level alignment.
No job restart or silent modification of native inputs is justified by this audit.

Detail-selection rule
Preserve a fact if omitting it would change at least one of:
- what biological distinction can be inferred;
- which molecular population is retained or represented;
- whether a downstream operation can accept the product;
- the actual documented difference or limitation between methods;
- interpretation of the reported performance comparison.
This is a rule for asking for relevant evidence, not an automatic proof of
completeness. A routine wash can be omitted from the architecture until a paper
shows its composition/order matters to the particular change. A novel condition
must be retained even when node names and gross input/output types do not change.

Exact research instruction to test next
'Identify the paper-supported differences, not just changed node names. For each
one, state what the earlier implementation did or contained, what the later one
does or contains, what is retained, and why the distinction matters to selection,
recovery, attribution, measurement or a demonstrated performance change. Locate
these facts in existing records when possible. Separate an explicit adopted
contribution from the baseline used for comparison. Check both sources before
asserting transfer. If the difference cannot be located or its consequence is
unproven, say precisely what is missing. Do not split a workflow or invent details
merely to satisfy a requested anchor. Controls and intermediate detection that
are integral to the assay route must not be confused with separate validation
experiments.'

Reference implementation audit and adopted/rejected choices
Intern-Atlas primary paper https://arxiv.org/html/2604.28158v1 and its official
local builder pinned 1185dc36555c547b5b6e9553779cb498a15ce587. The previous full audit
is experiments/reference_audits/intern_atlas_20261103/REPORT.txt. Reinspected
builder.py:llm_extract_paper/infer_relations/select_candidate_pairs/orient_pair
and evidence.py query helpers from the pinned local snapshot.
Adopt: type relations and deliver a bounded evidence package. Adapt: anchor claims
to a physical workflow and its molecular properties. Reject: treating normalized
names, temporal ranking or heuristic fallbacks as proof of methodological transfer.
The public local builder is not the full hosted system; no claim about unseen
hosted validation follows from its limitations.
Independent precedent: LabOP represents protocols as activities with connected
inputs/outputs and separate material/data handling; its official documentation
https://bioprotocols.github.io/labop/quickstart/ and repository
https://github.com/Bioprotocols/labop were consulted for the representation boundary.
Its purpose is protocol exchange/execution, not source-supported assay evolution;
its primitive library does not define a universal set of innovations. No LabOP
runtime was installed or tested. Prior EDC audit at commit
02aad17e2fc1e9845c84746b384e7fd3160edc1a supplies a separate retrieve-then-verify
normalization precedent; it was not rerun here. No external code was copied.
This scientific object is a set of evidenced relational claims, not a learned
estimator. Training-loss/sampler comparisons from the audit template do not apply.

Tests that would falsify the proposed revision
1. Regroup one source-supported procedure into two blocks: scientific difference
   should stay the same although the list of anchors changes.
2. Give two different routes the same final-library label: still recover the
   supported route difference; do not call them identical.
3. Keep Operation labels identical but change a relevant reagent or terminal
   group: recover the difference and its downstream interface.
4. Swap which molecule carries a tag: do not preserve a false capture mechanism.
5. Use a shared end property with a known confounder: do not infer exclusive target
   identity merely from the selected products.
6. Swap order across a required recording/separation step: flag the changed
   attribution/routing even when the set of operations is unchanged.
7. Supply a comparator citation with no adoption statement: no history edge.
8. Attribute a whole-method gain to one unisolated component: retain only the
   reported composite result; do not invent causal ablation evidence.
These are predeclared controls, not results. Existing source-anchor checks do not
establish that an LLM will pass them. Test on the current irCLIP result and a
contrasting method before expanding connection generation.

Agent use
Query an intended capability, inspect the corresponding measurement/selection
mechanism, follow a documented design change, and retrieve the exact boundaries
and failure limits needed to propose a transfer. The graph supplies precedent
and constraints; a proposed new combination remains a hypothesis requiring
verification. Path existence, similarity or a shared word does not validate it.
