Verdicts

1. Revised RNA-age candidate: mechanistically plausible, with a substantially
   better specified signal and identity path than the initial draft. No decisive
   chemical impossibility was found in the inspected sources. It is a proposed
   compatibility experiment, not a validated coassay or a demonstrated advance.

   Its path is 4sU-marked RNA -> on-bead conversion -> conventional NASC-style
   copying -> indexed RNA library. A separately released antibody-DNA reporter
   leaves in the first supernatant before conversion and receives its own indexed
   library in a mapped daughter well. The paired indices and realized well map
   preserve cell association. Revision removed the initial custom bead-bound
   cell-barcode/UMI library interface and uses established copying and late indexing.
   That removal reduces risk; it is not itself a new scientific contribution.

2. Physical-selection initial candidate: the EU-labelled RNA/cDNA-hybrid selection
   principle is credible and has direct precedent. The proposed new combination
   remains less completely specified. In particular, the bound hybrid -> released
   material -> sequencing library transition is delegated to an uninspected
   supplement. The exact release attachment path and realized transfer record also
   need clarification. These are evidence/specification gaps, not proof that a
   compatible mechanism cannot exist. No physical-selection revision was available
   locally, so this verdict must not be applied to an unseen revision.

3. Neither artifact establishes novelty or usefulness. Both use a closely related
   early reporter-routing idea, followed by different RNA measurements. Count the
   shared routing proposition once when assessing portfolio diversity. Originality,
   mechanistic coherence, and net measurement benefit require separate judgments.

Most consequential findings

A. Establish the bottleneck before claiming an improvement.
The strongest concern is the motivation for the extra split. The CITE-seq authors
report no observed competition between RNA and antibody oligos for polyT primers
in their tested settings [3]. Their updated protocol also says the cleavable linker
added no benefit to their original streptavidin-biotin architecture and could be
detrimental with direct conjugation [2]. These observations do not refute a new
routing use of release, or every possible competition regime. They do remove any
justification for treating either issue as an established general bottleneck.

The revised RNA-age draft appropriately makes its advantage conditional. Once
binding has been encoded in reporter DNA plus well identity, retaining native
antibody activity during RNA chemistry need not be necessary. The relevant question
is whether reporter information or RNA recovery is actually lost. A native-antibody
denaturation argument alone would not establish this.

B. Known components do not establish the integrated assay, but context transfers
are legitimate hypotheses.
NASC-seq demonstrates on-bead conversion, modified Smart-seq2 copying, tagmentation
and indexing [1]. The revised late-index RNA path follows that architecture. It
does not establish compatibility with the proposed release chemistry and reporter
split. Importantly, the original NASC bulk chemistry includes DTT before IAA and
a later quench: a categorical “any residual reductant makes this impossible” rule
would be incorrect. Release-reagent carryover is a quantitative context question.

Original CITE-seq methods support the mixed noncovalent biotin-streptavidin and
covalent reducible DNA-linker architecture [4]. The revised draft distinguishes
these bonds correctly. A custom non-poly(A) reporter and release construct still
need actual characterization; familiar chemistry is not proof of the complete
product or its recovery.

Official Feature Barcoding documentation already separates the protein-tag capture
sequence from RNA oligo(dT) capture [5]. Orthogonal reporter capture is therefore
an established component, not the proposed novelty. SPARC demonstrates that a
single-cell lysate can be separated into bead-captured RNA and a protein-containing
supernatant, followed by different readouts [6]. Its native-protein preservation
requirements cannot automatically be imported into an already DNA-encoded reporter
assay. Neither precedent establishes this metabolic-label combination.

C. Physical-selection concerns need calibrated wording.
scEU-seq explicitly places a cell barcode, UMI and T7-containing library sequence
on cDNA, pools RNA/cDNA hybrids, then uses EU-derived biotin on RNA for affinity
selection [7]. Thus transient hybrid linkage and pooling are demonstrated principles,
not intrinsically invalid steps. spinDrop further describes an adapted nascent-RNA
selection implementation [8]. Neither establishes the draft's low-input, off-cell
click chemistry after reporter capture/copying. Whether that changed preparation
preserves hybrids and RNA yield remains testable, not settled negatively.

The physical draft's operation ps_make_rna_libraries does not trace the required
strands and handles through the downstream library block. Retrieve the referenced
supplement and specify that transition before calling this a complete design. Its
ps_manifest describes planned pairing, while ps_route lacks a realized transfer
record. Planned mapping cannot demonstrate that each transfer actually occurred.
The RNA-age revision has already made this distinction explicit.

D. What can actually be inferred?
The RNA-age revision correctly limits its endpoint to labelled-window RNA surviving
to lysis and represented in recovered reads. Without UMIs, read support is not a
count of independent original molecules. Sparse convertible sites, background
substitutions and first-copy errors limit new/old inference. A single pulse does
not separately identify synthesis and degradation rates. NASC's tested cold-handling
control also means continued labelling during handling must be calibrated in the
actual setting, not asserted as an inevitable fatal effect [1].

For physical selection, bound reads represent recovered labelled RNA; unbound is
not necessarily pure old RNA. Recovery and capture efficiency must be calibrated
before interpreting fractions as biological abundance. Reporter-copy UMIs count
copying events unless a one-to-one original-reporter relationship is established;
they do not automatically count original proteins.

E. Compare against a real alternative and use the right denominator.
The revised RNA-age draft has a stronger comparison than the initial shared-polyA
baseline: early routing versus a specified orthogonal reporter co-processing/late
separation architecture. However, that comparison arm is also engineered and must
work before a superiority claim is interpretable. RNA-only and reporter-only
controls distinguish integration loss from biological signal. A plate-based
index-sort fluorescence plus RNA-age sequencing approach is a legitimate simpler
comparator under the user's broad measurement goal; its exact combination is not
claimed here to have been demonstrated. The goal does not require every modality
to be read by sequencing.

Report joint usable profiles per input cell at comparable total sequencing effort
and false-call criteria, with component recovery and costs visible. Restricting the
denominator to surviving paired profiles could turn extra losses into apparent
quality gains. Species mixing cannot detect every swap between cells of the same
species; known per-well identities are needed for that stronger linkage claim.

Smallest decisive checks, ordered by decision value
These are experimental questions, not an executable protocol.

1. RNA-age motivation: does the actual reporter product lose recoverable identity
   when exposed to the intended RNA chemistry, compared with matched unexposed
   material? If not, chemistry isolation is not yet a demonstrated advantage.
2. Integrated mass balance: with distinguishable RNA and reporters, compare early
   routing to the fair orthogonal baseline, counting retained material, transferred
   supernatant, washes, failed cells and wrong pairings. Ask whether there is a net
   gain in joint measurement, not just a cleaner surviving fraction.
3. RNA inference: label/no-label and conversion/no-conversion controls plus known
   mixtures should establish false-new calls and uncertainty at the intended
   coverage. PCR-derived read multiplicity cannot be treated as independent truth.
4. Physical selection: first recover and specify the published downstream library
   block. Then test whether known labelled/unlabelled hybrids bearing different
   cell identities retain the correct selection relationship through the proposed
   changed processing and pooling. Compare with unpooled material.
5. Library handoff: demonstrate that both physical-selection fractions yield the
   intended cell, target and fraction identities in their library products. This
   resolves a missing implementation link rather than testing a broad claim that
   RNA/cDNA hybrid selection is impossible.

Harness consequence
Review output should distinguish an absent mechanism, a demonstrated incompatibility,
an explicitly specified but untested transfer, and an unsupported benefit. Every
review claim needs its evidence and scope. Calling all of these “blocking” without
the distinction obscures whether the next action is source recovery, design
clarification, a decisive experiment, or rejection. An access failure is not a
negative scientific result. A complete, source-grounded draft remains a hypothesis
until its integrated measurement and advantage are established.
