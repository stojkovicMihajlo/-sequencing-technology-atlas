All three recover this route:
Cells + barcoded primer beads -> co-encapsulation in droplets -> cell lysis and
poly(A)-RNA capture on beads -> droplet breakage and bead pooling -> bulk reverse
transcription/amplification -> DNA-derived single-cell sequence readout.

All three preserve the same consequential interpretations:
- RNA capture occurs before pooling and before reverse transcription.
- Bead association preserves provenance when droplets are broken.
- The bead cell barcode is shared among its primers; UMIs vary among primers.
- Barcode/UMI information accompanies transcript-derived cDNA.
- The physical sequencing substrate is DNA; the represented analyte is mRNA.
- Paired reads supply barcode/UMI and transcript-derived sequence information.
- The library reports captured polyadenylated RNA near transcript 3-prime ends.
- Doublets and ambient RNA limit single-cell attribution.

Differences that matter for the harness
1. Website and RNA-poster authored fields explicitly describe template switching
   and the additional PCR handle. The single-cell output is more compressed and
   records reverse transcription/amplification without that extra explanation.
2. Website discusses the catalogue's handle-replacement wording in the cDNA
   Operation description. RNA poster also records a formal Correction object.
   Thus the same issue is not always retrievable from the same structured field.
3. Null mechanism fields differ:0for website,2for single-cell,3for RNA poster.
   The corresponding transformations may still be explained elsewhere in the
   record. This is annotation variability, not proof of contradictory chemistry.
4. Inputs differ: only website and RNA poster received collection page110, which
   contains the disputed handle-replacement statement. Correction coverage must
   be interpreted relative to those supplied sources.

Interpretation
This is positive evidence of consistency in the final workflow structure and
main scientific content across the three depictions. Detailed annotation is less
uniform. The RNA-poster pipeline needed a revision, so final agreement does not
establish equal first-pass success. All three share the same original primary
paper and closely related Illumina drawings; agreement here does not establish
correctness across different technologies. No fresh expert/wet-lab review occurred.

Use this as an archived development case for source consolidation and consistent
fact placement. Do not count it as a measured LLM identity-resolver benchmark.
