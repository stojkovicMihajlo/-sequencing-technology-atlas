Published supplement: relevant findings
--------------------------------------
Page numbers below are one-based PDF page positions.

S2.2, pp. 22–24: modified PaperQA2 uses original retrieved passages for final
answer generation instead of their intermediate summaries. Its demonstration
found context conflation between TdT validation and multibase synthesis, and a
missing buffer formulation. Separate document identifiers avoid collisions when
papers and supplements share a DOI. This is a bounded retrieval comparison,
not a universal accuracy guarantee.

S2.3, pp. 26–27: procedures use a part/step/option hierarchy and reagent checklist.
S2.4, p. 31: reagent matching distinguishes a buffer from its separately available
constituents and requires composition agreement for specified recipes.

S9.2, pp. 167–168: authors report ten repetitions of the same synthesis prompt;
60% yielded complete synthesis protocols that successfully produced the intended
sequence. This is their reported compound plan/completion/outcome criterion,
not extraction accuracy, per-operation correctness, or a broad task success rate.
The section does not separately enumerate causes of all four unsuccessful cases.

S9.3, pp. 168–169: of 14 observed hardware-error events, six required operator
correction. Recovery depends on observable error signals. Thus the abstract's
autonomy wording should not be expanded into a claim of zero intervention or
uniform reliability.

Literature.py uses targeted questions over a local modified PaperQA collection and
returns file IDs for sharing results. Its cache is keyed by experiment name, so
that implementation should not be copied as source/query-sensitive memoization.

Protocol.py selects initialization, refinement, inventory adjustment or optimization
using input/history metadata. The protocol prompt prescribes part/step/option
organization. Some prompt variants remove a step when no reagent option remains
and assume unlisted reagents available; those are unsuitable defaults for faithful
historical extraction. Their existence is not evidence that a particular reported
experiment failed because of these instructions.

Implications for this atlas — engineering judgments, not paper findings
---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Use compact summaries for discovery, but original source passages and explicit
   experimental context for admitting workflow details or historical changes.
2. Keep document identity separate from method identity. Separate supplements must
   remain retrievable without scheduling the same method as independent work.
3. Distinguish source-supported descriptions, proposed adaptations and experimentally
   validated outcomes. Source/model review cannot establish wet-lab performance.
4. AutoDNA's atomic services describe instrument capabilities, not a universal
   ontology of chemical primitives. Do not replace our scientific transformations
   with hardware commands or infer chemical interchangeability from common purpose.
5. Their study does not establish historical edge types or a data-derived change
   taxonomy. Our citation-supported change extraction and subsequent grouping still
   require their own evidence and evaluation.

No atlas schema, prompts, workflows or running jobs were changed by this audit.
