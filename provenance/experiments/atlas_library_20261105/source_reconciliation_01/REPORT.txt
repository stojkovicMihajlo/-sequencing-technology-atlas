WHY THE POPULATIONS DIFFER
1. Repeated method depictions: Drop-seq has a website card, RNA-poster row and
   single-cell row. G&T-seq has a card and rows on all three posters. These are
   sources for a method, not automatically three/four independent implementations.
2. Grouped headings: 25 website titles have slash-separated names. For example,
   ChIP-Seq/ChIP-exo/HT-ChIP. Some names are aliases, some distinct methods; the
   source heading alone cannot decide applicability or whether to merge them.
3. Additional methods: CITE-Seq, ECCITE-Seq, sci-RNA-seq and SPLiT-seq are named
   on the single-cell poster without matching website headings in this snapshot.
   Lack of a website heading does not exclude these from the target inventory.
4. Different spellings: ART-Seq versus ARTseq, ChIRP-Seq versus ChIRP, and
   Paired-end RAD-Seq versus PE RAD-Seq nominate identity checks. Absence of an
   exact string match is not proof that one source lacks that technology.
5. Different resource classes: footer panels include commercial preparations and
   sequencing-mechanism explanations. They need coverage and classification;
   they should not silently inflate a research-assay count.

WHAT THE LABEL QUEUE MEANS
Conservative case/spacing/punctuation normalization and the registered heading
members yield 307 main-source heading keys: 165 present on website and poster,
120 poster-only exact headings, and 22 website-only exact headings. These are NOT
307 technologies or 120 genuinely new methods. Each row retains literal names,
source IDs, grouped-heading membership and unreviewed identity status. Aliases,
different scopes and homonyms still need content-based review. Footer-only names
are separately visible. No prior model's extracted method_names define this set.

All 478 occurrence IDs are distinct; duplicate IDs or unknown resource classes
fail the inventory command. New extraction jobs are not generated by this report.
One website card (MDA/IMS-MDA/MIDAS) lacks its original heading in the archive and
uses its archived recovered title; title_basis records the missing original.
47 website records lack category metadata in this archive. Do not interpret that
as no category on Illumina's site or exclude them from RNA/joint-assay screening.

DIRECT POSTER INSPECTION — 5 November follow-up
The engineering agent inspected full-page renders of all three original PDFs,
then five high-resolution crops: both Drop-seq rows and all three G&T-seq rows.
The existing selectable text/word-coordinate indexes were used for locations
and bibliographic checks, not model-generated workflow prose.

These are overlapping editorial collections, not mutually exclusive method sets.
RNA (current as of 10 November 2016): RNA Transcription, RNA-Protein Interactions,
RNA Low-Level Detection, RNA Modifications and RNA Structure.
DNA (current as of 10 November 2016): DNA Rearrangements and Markers, DNA Low-Level
Detection, Epigenetics, DNA-Protein Interactions and Protein-Protein Interactions.
Single-Cell (current as of 14 April 2020): RNA Low-Level Detection, DNA Low-Level
Detection and Integrated Techniques. This later collection repeats older methods
and adds methods not present in the two earlier posters.
The paper dates above describe poster editions, not invention dates.

Drop-seq: RNA poster, RNA Low-Level Detection (continued in third column), and
single-cell poster, RNA Low-Level Detection (first column). Both drawings show
barcoded beads, droplets, lysis/capture, pooled beads, cDNA synthesis/amplification
and sequencing. The captions and arrangement match at the depicted method level;
layout/line wrapping differs. Both cite Macosko et al. (2015), Cell 161:1202-1214.
Decision: one depicted Drop-seq implementation, two poster locations. These do
not justify two protocol-extraction jobs. Retain the 2020 single-cell drawing as
one representative poster source, with the 2016 row as a redundant location.

G&T-seq: RNA poster, RNA Low-Level Detection; DNA poster, DNA Low-Level Detection;
single-cell poster, Integrated Techniques. All three show single-cell isolation,
lysis, streptavidin magnetic-bead RNA capture, separated RNA/DNA branches,
Smart-seq2/MDA amplification, sequencing and matched RNA/genome analysis. The 2020
row explicitly labels polyA RNA where older rows say RNA (and depict poly(A)).
All cite Macaulay et al. (2015), Nat Methods 12:519-522. No changed branch or
amplification strategy is depicted in these copies.
Decision: one depicted G&T-seq implementation, three poster locations. Retain the
2020 drawing and its explicit polyA label; older rows are redundant depictions,
not separate assay versions. Preserve all original PDFs and source locations.

These two consolidations are based on direct diagram comparison, headings and
bibliography, not only matching names. They do not establish deduplication of
every other row. Website descriptions remain useful additional source content;
we have not called their entire contents redundant merely because their diagrams
may overlap. No harness, extraction artifact or running job was changed here.
