Usability and graph review — 4 November 2026

Result
The current selection contains 19 model-reviewed schematic workflows. It is
usable for retrieving assay architecture, examining inputs/outputs and information
carriers, and selecting connected groups for source-grounded comparison. It is
not an executable wet-lab protocol collection or a completed evolutionary graph.

Graph output
current_atlas.json selects the 17 unchanged workflows and the two newly reviewed
replacements, preserving their exact local records and provenance. It contains
19 workflows, 166 Operations, 227 States and 377 material/data Flows. Including
scope, correction, bibliography and containment records: 628 nodes, 1,153 edges.
Shared publications are joined by verified PMID only; operation names never
create scientific equivalence or historical edges. The selection and both parent
graph hashes are recorded in current_graph_receipt.json and the graph metadata.
All 393 operation/state lookups were exercised. Operation input/output lookups
agree with their declared States; connected-group retrieval succeeds and keeps
boundary records. Query examples demonstrate irCLIP RNA recovery-to-library and
icSHAPE RT/capture/release. Search remains lexical candidate retrieval, not a
semantic matching algorithm or evidence of shared mechanisms.

This is a current batch graph view. It has not been canonically merged into the
legacy 201-technology atlas, and its new records have not been added to the older
interactive website. No evolutionary relations were generated by either batch.
The earlier Drop-seq history pilot remains separate; its links are not silently
attached to these records. References_publication means a source association,
not that one assay evolved from another.

Useful scientific distinctions present in the records
- DR-Seq versus G&T/scM&T: joint material processing versus physical RNA/DNA
  separation and independent downstream branches are retained.
- icSHAPE: the treated RNA carries adduct-linked biotin; the DMSO control obtains
  its capture handle through a different linker. Primary protocol 26766114,
  passage b00215, establishes that distinction. RNA-associated cDNA capture is
  followed by RNA-dependent release; the graph does not put the RNA adduct on DNA.
- irCLIP: the later cDNA purification is biotin/streptavidin capture of a hybrid,
  followed by cDNA release. It is not a second antibody immunoprecipitation.
  Primary 27111506 supplies the hybrid-capture and elution passages.
- miCLIP-m6A: antibody-dependent truncation and substitution readouts are kept
  separate, and the primary 254-nm condition corrects the catalogue's 365-nm
  caption. The methyl group is not depicted as transferred to the cDNA reporter.

Remaining restrictions
G&T-seq, GMUCT 2.0, ICE and miR-CLIP have abstract-only original architecture
papers in these registrations. Their diagram-supported architecture is useful;
specific primary-method chemistry, interfaces and historical claims need fuller
evidence before use. Other fulltexts in the same bundle are not substitutes for
the correct original implementation.

Nine nonblocking unknowns remain across six methods, including barcode layout,
fragment retention, primer assignment, selection specificity and miR-CLIP's
affinity-to-RT interface. Carry these into dependent comparisons/design work.
For example, miR-CLIP does not establish whether RT follows support release or
crosslink reversal; the graph must not be treated as evidence for either route.

Detail varies by source and scope: five operations can describe a concise
chemistry route, while 19 or 21 operations retain treated/control or antibody
branches. Operation counts and equal labels are not an equivalence criterion.
This review found no additional blocker in the checked records beyond the two
now-repaired cases. Passing these checks does not prove absence of other errors.

Next graph-building work
Use these records as workflow anchors. Follow primary-paper citations and exact
method explanations to establish what was inherited and changed, then attach
reviewed claims to the relevant Operations or connected groups. Keep shared
purpose, shared mechanism and historical development distinct. Do not infer a
development edge merely because two methods share RT, ligation or a citation.
