TASK: Find and explain source-supported operation correspondences for assay design.

This is an optional discovery-stage instruction, not part of every extraction or
review prompt. The caller supplies the graph path and research need. Use the graph
CLI and its structured JSON responses. Do not edit source graphs during discovery.

1. State the property you want to compare. Examples of dimensions are assay
function, transformation mechanism, information carrier, or an interface requirement.
Use your own question. `atlas-graph roles` offers optional broad search questions;
they are overlapping lenses, not mandatory stages or a complete scientific taxonomy.
Sample preparation is a function. Chemistry can implement preparation, capture,
encoding or readout. Keep these aspects separate. Do not assign a role from a search
score, force every operation into a role, or infer order from the order of the roles.

2. Use `atlas-graph discover-operations GRAPH --question 'PROPERTY OR QUESTION'`.
Optionally add --workflow WORKFLOW_ID and repeated --operation OPERATION_ID for
source context, or --role ROLE_ID. A wide selection may obscure a local purpose;
inspect the operations and state a focal question instead of repeatedly dumping
more context into the search. The optional semantic mode retrieves candidates,
including false positives. Names, retrieval scores and non-hits prove nothing.

3. Inspect candidate inputs, action, outputs, information effects, conditions,
resources, variants, unknowns and neighbors. Operations are source-specific records.
A useful comparison unit may contain several connected operations and can cross
the author's local module boundaries. Group sizes may differ between methods.
Do not cut an action out of a bundled operation unless its source supports the
refinement. New labels are not necessary for every contextual difference.

4. Use `atlas-graph correspondences GRAPH WORKFLOW_ID --operation OPERATION_ID`
with repeated --operation arguments to read existing scoped comparisons and concept
memberships. Read differences and not-established claims as well as shared claims.
Direct claim targets show where a claim points; they do not prove every action in
that operation matches. Untargeted context is not inherited correspondence, and a
concept membership for a group is not automatically a membership for every subset.
Use the existing claims, inspect and source commands for original evidence.

5. For a new comparison, use prepare-comparison with exact connected selections,
shared_purpose or shared_mechanism, and the question you actually investigated.
The existing source reader and independent reviewer assess the proposed claims.
Source support, historical development and experimental substitution are separate
questions. Keep unsupported transfer hypotheses explicit. Do not create an ancestry
edge from similarity, or assert compatibility merely because a mechanism is shared.

Return the focal question, exact workflow/operation IDs, source-backed shared claims,
consequential differences, required interfaces and unresolved properties. Report
when retrieval or available evidence was insufficient. No label quota, required
number of matches, or complete atlas coverage is assumed.
